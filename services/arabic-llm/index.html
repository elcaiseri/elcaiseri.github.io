Arabic LLM and Arabic RAG development

Most LLM tools are built and tested in English. Arabic brings its own problems: Modern Standard Arabic next to Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine dialects, Arabic and English mixed in one sentence, several valid spellings of the same word, and right-to-left documents that confuse standard parsers. A system that scores well in English can quietly fail your Arabic-speaking users.

Where Arabic breaks standard pipelines

Spelling variants

The same word can be written several ways: different alef and hamza forms (أ إ آ ا), taa marbuta or haa (ة / ه), alef maqsura or yaa (ى / ي), with or without diacritics. People searching for my own name type both إسلام and اسلام. Without normalization, search misses obvious matches.

Dialects and code-switching

Users write in dialect, switch to English for technical terms, or type Arabic in Latin letters. Retrieval and prompts have to handle all of it, and the evaluation has to include it.

Tokenization and cost

Many models split Arabic text into more tokens than the same content in English. That raises cost and latency and reduces how much fits in the context window, so the choice of model and embedding model matters more than it does in English.

Documents and speech

Scanned PDFs, mixed-direction tables and OCR noise need cleanup before indexing. For audio, Whisper large-v3 transcribes Arabic, but accuracy varies with dialect and domain vocabulary, so transcripts have to be checked against real recordings.

How I choose and test models

Step 1

Build an Arabic test set

Real questions and documents from your domain, in Modern Standard Arabic and dialect, including mixed Arabic–English text. Not English questions run through a translator.

Step 2

Compare models on it

Open-weight multilingual models such as Qwen, LLaMA and Mistral when data must stay on your servers; API models such as GPT, Claude and Gemini when it's allowed to leave; and Arabic-first models such as ALLaM or Jais when they fit. Embedding models are compared the same way.

Step 3

Tune retrieval for Arabic

Text normalization, chunking, hybrid search and re-ranking, each measured on the Arabic test set rather than assumed to carry over from English.

Step 4

Ship and keep measuring

Deployment on your cloud or your own servers, with feedback from Arabic-speaking users feeding the next round of tests.

Government entities, banks, healthcare providers and large enterprises often need data to stay inside the Kingdom and inside their own network. I deploy open-weight language models on infrastructure you control, so your teams get generative AI without that data leaving it.

I've led on-premise deployments of LLaMA and Qwen models on GPU Kubernetes for large Saudi enterprises, with RAG assistants and Whisper speech-to-text running on the same platform.

How a deployment runs

Step 1

Requirements and sizing

Use cases, number of users, response-time targets and data classification. From those I size the models and GPUs, and decide whether a single server is enough or a Kubernetes cluster is needed.

Step 2

Model selection

Candidate models are tested on your data, including Arabic if your users need it. Quantization and batching are tuned so the chosen model fits your hardware and budget.

Step 3

Deployment

Containers on Kubernetes or a GPU server, vLLM serving, authentication, logging and integration with your identity and document systems. Offline installation is possible for networks with no internet access.

Step 4

Operations and handover

Monitoring of latency, GPU usage and answer quality; model updates through CI/CD with GitHub Actions and MLflow; and documentation and training so your team can run it.

On-premise, in-Kingdom cloud or external API?

Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and sector regulators set rules on where personal data can be stored and processed. Your legal and compliance teams decide what applies to you; I build the system to meet it.

On-premise

Best when data can't leave your network, usage is steady and heavy, or you need full control over models and updates. You provide or buy the GPU servers.

Cloud region in Saudi Arabia

Data stays in the Kingdom without running your own hardware. Good for pilots and changing workloads, if your policies allow a cloud provider.

External API

The fastest start and the strongest frontier models, for data that is allowed to leave. Often a good way to build a prototype before moving on-premise.

Common questions

What hardware do we need?

It depends on model size, number of users and the response time you need. A pilot can run on a single GPU server; production usually runs on a small GPU cluster. I size it from your requirements before you buy anything.

Are open-weight models good enough?

For most enterprise assistants and search, yes, especially with RAG over your own documents. I test them on your tasks next to an API model, so you see the difference before you decide.

Does it support Arabic?

Yes. Multilingual open-weight models handle Arabic, and I evaluate them on Arabic test sets from your own domain.
